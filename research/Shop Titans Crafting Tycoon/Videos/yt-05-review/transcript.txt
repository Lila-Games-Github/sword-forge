While scrolling through Steam's huge
list of free games, one title caught my
interest. It looked familiar, but I
couldn't remember why. Then it hit me. I
played this on my phone way back in
2019, but now it's on Steam. That got me
thinking. Can a mobile game really stand
out on Steam when it's next to so many
full-blown PC games? Let's find out by
taking a look at Shop Titans. Before I
talk about how easy it is to get into
this game, I have to be honest with you.
I usually review games that I've never
played before so I can give them all a
fair shot. In this case though, I played
Shop Titans 5 years ago. I even
redownloaded the mobile app just to
check. And believe it or not, I played
it longer during this review than I did
back then. I didn't even remember the
gameplay from the first time. So, this
review is still fresh and fair. But I
just wanted to be clear since that's
always my goal with these videos. Now,
when you start Shop Titans, the tutorial
does two things. It shows you the story
and it teaches you how to run your shop.
It feels a lot like other mobile game
tutorials. It tells you how to do
something, gives you a little time to
try it out, then jumps back in to
explain the next step. According to the
App Store, the game is for ages 9 and
up, and that makes sense. The long,
simple explanations feel like they're
made for younger players. Not only for
them, but they're clearly part of the
audience. I'd say your first 20 minutes
or so are spent learning how the shop
works while slowly getting into the
story. Playing Shop Titans with friends
doesn't feel like a regular PC game. It
feels more like a mobile game. Right
before you hit level four, the game
opens up guilds and in-game chat. When
you join a guild, you can help others
and ask for items, too. But if you want
to play with your actual friends and not
just random people, you'll need to get
into the same guild. That can be tricky
if there aren't enough spots left. You
can make your own guild, but there is a
catch. Starting a guild costs 500 gems.
That's about $5 in real money. You can
earn gems by playing, but it takes a
long time. Probably hours just to get
enough to play with your friends. So,
unless you want to grind a lot, playing
with friends might cost you. Thanks to
the tutorial and clicking around the
crafting screen, the main goal is clear.
Make better gear and sell it. The game
also gives you quests which teach you
what to do next. They show you that you
should keep upgrading your shop, your
workers, and just about everything else
as you go. Some quests also just give
you rewards. As your shop gets better,
you earn champion coins to level up your
champions, and that's what keeps the
story moving forward. This game starts
to feel a bit like wash, rinse, repeat,
because that's exactly what it is. While
playing Shop Titans, the main loop never
really changes. You craft items, you
sell them, then you do it all over
again. The only real thing that you do
is manage your heroes and send them on
quests. The story doesn't add much
either. Every time your champions level
up, you get a tiny bit of story, but
honestly, it's not that interesting. For
example, one character named Argon wants
to become one of the king's knights. So,
when you help him level up, his story
moves forward. But there's barely any
detail, and it doesn't feel connected to
what you're doing in your shop. Instead
of a big exciting story, it feels more
like a small side plot that doesn't
really matter. At the end of the day,
Shop Titans is kind of like a fancy
clicker game. You click, wait, click
again, and there's no big goal or
exciting story to pull you along. That
said, Shop Titans has a lot of replay
value. The game runs in seasons, and
like most live service games, there's a
season pass that gives you extra
challenges. The setup is really common
in online games today. Whether or not
that content is actually fun is another
story, and we'll get to that soon. But
first, let's take a look at how many
people are playing Shop Titans. When I
logged on, there were over 4,500 people
playing Shop Titans on Steam. The
24-hour peak was just over 4600. That's
actually a pretty good number
considering that's only Steam players.
The game is also available on iOS,
Android, and the Epic Game Store, so the
total number of players could be much
higher. One website, playtracker.net,
says that there are more than 33,000
active players in total. But when I
looked into how they came up with that
number, it wasn't very clear. So, take
that with a grain of salt. Looking back
at the Steam stats, player numbers have
slowly gone down over the past year,
just a little bit. But overall, the
player base still looks strong. Even
with the slight drop, there's nothing
major to worry about for now. If you've
seen some of my past reviews, you know
I'm not a big fan of games that make you
create an account just to play. Luckily,
Shop Titans doesn't force that. Instead,
linking an account is just a feature,
something you can do, not something you
have to do. And honestly, that makes
sense. I don't think most people are
discovering this game on Steam and
sitting down for long PC sessions. more
likely they started playing on mobile
and just want the option to jump on
their PC when it's convenient. The Steam
player count shows that there is a solid
group playing there, but it's still much
smaller than the 33,000 that we saw from
Play Tracker. If that number's accurate,
it backs up the idea that most players
probably are on mobile. Either way, it's
nice that you can play on different
devices and pick up where you left off.
Now, here's something surprising. Shop
Titans looks pretty good, even though it
has very low system requirements.
Seriously, you could probably run this
on a potato. The art style is simple and
clean, and the game is easy on the eyes.
The menus are also laid out really well,
so you don't get lost clicking around.
Since this game started on mobile, I was
a little worried that the PC version
would feel like a bad port of an old
mobile game, but that wasn't the case. I
was surprised to see that you can earn
power back by giving discounts, chatting
with customers, or selling items, which
keeps you playing. You can also get
power back by adding a pet or decorating
your shop. When a customer interacts
with decorations or your pet, you'll
earn power points back. In the early
parts of the game, you can play for a
long time without needing to stop as
long as you're careful about how often
you use sir charges to earn more gold.
For some context, searchcharges use a
lot of power, but you get a lot more
gold in return. Given the mobile nature
of the game, I would expect that at some
point power is going to be harder to
acquire, and searchcharging your items
will have a bigger impact. Aside from
those features, there really isn't much
else in Shop Titans that stood out as
cool or exciting. And there are a few
reasons why it doesn't feel that
exciting. First, the story is weak and
honestly kind of pointless. Remember
Argon, the guy who wants to be one of
the king's knights? Well, after my time
playing, the only real thing I learned
was he wants to be a knight. Later,
there's a short scene where someone
teases him about having a crush on the
king's assistant, but that's it. It
didn't pull me in at all, and it
definitely didn't make me want to keep
playing for the story. Now, let's talk
about the gem problem again. I really
don't like when games make you pay for
basic features like playing with your
friends. Yes, you can earn 500 gems by
grinding, but you'll also need gems for
other important things. For example, if
you want to open chests and you don't
have keys, you'll need to spend gems.
So, in the end, it starts to feel like
you have to spend real money. What makes
it worse is that the game wants you to
use guilds to get help from other
players. But, if you want to play with
your actual friends, you either need to
find the same guild with open spots or
pay gems to create your own. That just
doesn't feel fair. Playing with friends
should be simple and built into any game
claiming to be multiplayer. But it's not
just the gems. The microtransactions in
this game are way over the top. It feels
like every time you log in, there's a
new offer trying to get you to spend
money. The store icon is always showing
and you're constantly reminded to check
out new packs. At one point, even the
king showed up at my shop asking me to
buy stuff. I get that free games need to
make money, but this kind of pressure is
one of the reasons I stopped playing
mobile games. And seeing this on Steam
just feels wrong. Another big problem is
how many different types of currencies
the game uses. It's way too much. You've
got gold, gems, spin tickets, champion
coins, guild coins, renown, fortune
tokens, and maybe even more. I didn't
notice. It's confusing. The game would
be a lot easier to manage if it just
used one regular currency, one for
leveling, and maybe one paid currency.
But with so many, it just feels messy
and hard to keep track of. Now, you
might be thinking, "This game has a lot
of problems. Why are you still talking
about it?" And honestly, it's because
Shop Titans is kind of fun. I've said
before, I don't really play mobile games
anymore. I I got tired of all the ads,
pop-ups, and being asked to spend money
every 5 minutes. I ended up deleting
those games. These days, the only kind
of mobile games I play is something like
Stardew Valley, a full game I paid for
with no annoying extras. But even though
Shop Titans is clearly a mobile game at
heart, I still enjoyed my time with it.
It's sort of a mix between an idol game
and a clicker. You don't really earn
anything while you're away except for
items crafting in the background, and
when you're playing, you mostly just
click to make stuff. But there's
something relaxing about it. It's the
kind of game you can play while drinking
a cup of coffee, then walk away and come
back later. It's not super exciting, but
it's kind of cozy. But all that said, I
don't think there's enough here to make
me come back. I totally get why some
people would enjoy this game, especially
mobile gamers. and the ability to play
across different devices is a nice
feature. But when you compare Shop
Titans to all the other free games
available on PC, it's tough to
recommend. That's ultimately why Shop
