## Core loop actions

- Store items -> converts bin slots to increase per-rack storage capacity (a rack holds ~4 items per space) so fewer total storage furniture pieces are needed once racks replace bins.
- Small Talk -> chance-based interaction with a customer; success lets the shop Sell (or Surcharge/Discount) without needing to Suggest an item manually.
- Suggest / Discount / Surcharge / Refuse / Sell / Wait are the standing customer-interaction verbs in every negotiation popup.
- Crafting queue: up to several concurrent craft slots shown as circular icons with countdown timers (e.g., 13m32s / 11m34s / 9m42s / 3m36s) plus a "Craft" button; slot count scales with which bins/benches are active.
- Furniture upgrade path: start timer -> guild members can each assist to cut time -> finish instantly by spending gems if not fully guild-assisted.

## Shop layout / decoration systems (endgame-specific)

- Three concrete endgame shop-layout archetypes described:
  1. **All-bins (production-heavy):** many bins out, all tier-1 resources 600+, tier-2 near 200; supports high concurrent crafting (~12 slots) but caps energy lower (~4,958) and requires manually storing everything to switch configurations (no bulk-convert button).
  2. **Hybrid "one of each bin" (max shop):** one of each bin type kept out alongside racks; ~7,000 energy while still able to craft ~4 items concurrently; described as "what most top players are going to have."
  3. **All-racks (sell-speed-heavy):** every bin converted to racks/chests; projected ~8,000-8,200 max energy at full (level 15) furniture upgrades; enables surcharging/selling every item instantly off small-talk success with zero crafting; trades away all crafting (and Legendary chance) for maximum net-worth-per-minute via fast selling.
- Racks explicitly hold more per placed furniture piece than bins (a rack's each "space" holds ~4 items), so converting from bins to racks needs fewer total storage furniture in the shop while raising the energy ceiling - the core mechanical lever behind all three archetypes.
- Reducing chest count (used for carrying components/fodder like a "chance" or extra components) is called out as a further energy-ceiliing lever once the player has enough pre-made items ready to sell and no longer needs chest-based fodder storage.
- Open vs. closed shop floor-plan is called a distinct, separate design axis from bins/racks: a closed/limited layout restricts which shelves a customer can reach, so they pick items faster (less walking) and let the shop process more customers per minute, at the cost of visual scale; an open layout looks better/bigger but customers take longer to choose and a too-small effective space can trigger a "too many customers" overflow message that makes customers leave. Creator ran open-concept for most of the game purely for aesthetics and only considered closing it up once optimizing purely for net-worth speed.
- Furniture upgrade system (seen on Mannequin, implied same for all storage furniture): per-level timer, up to 5 guild members can each contribute an assist that roughly halves total remaining time, remainder can be gem-rushed ("Finish now"); full level-15 max upgrades are gold/gem-expensive enough that the creator hadn't done it yet even at ~900M net worth.
- Furniture right-click/select menu: Skins (cosmetic reskin), Upgrade, Storage (capacity/config), Edit (move/rotate) - Skins confirmed as a distinct decorative layer separate from the capacity-upgrade path.

## 5 key findings

- Endgame shop layout is fundamentally a 3-way tradeoff the game does not resolve for the player: all-bins (max concurrent crafting, ~4,958 energy cap, Legendary chance) vs hybrid one-of-each-bin (~7,000 energy, still crafts, "what most top players run") vs all-racks (~8,000-8,200 projected energy, zero crafting, pure fast-sell/surcharge net-worth grinding) - a strong signal that any teardown/economy model needs an explicit late-game "storage furniture allocation" lever, not just a single energy stat.
- Racks are strictly denser storage than bins (~4 items per rack "space" vs bin capacity), making rack conversion the core energy-ceiling lever; there is no in-game bulk-convert action, so switching configurations is a manual, one-by-one "Store" grind the creator calls "painful" - a real friction point worth flagging for a competitor UX improvement (a "convert all" or saved-layout-preset feature would be a clear differentiator).
- Small Talk success functionally removes the need to Discount fodder items and lets a high-energy shop Surcharge/Sell every item instantly back-to-back - meaning at sufficient energy, selling speed alone (not crafting output) can become the dominant net-worth engine, decoupling "endgame progress" from the core crafting loop the early/mid game is built around.
- Shop floor-plan openness (open vs. closed) is presented as an orthogonal design axis to the bins/racks decision: closed layouts measurably speed up customer item-selection (less walking, more controllable choices, higher customer throughput) at the cost of visual/spatial appeal, while open layouts risk a "too many customers" overflow/leave state if the effective space is too small - a concrete lever for teardown analysis of spatial/queueing design in shop-management games.
- Furniture upgrade pacing intentionally forces a guild-plus-gems hybrid strategy: full gem-rushing every level is implied to be prohibitively expensive, so the intended (and demonstrated) late-game pattern is "wait for up to 5 guild assists, then gem-rush only the remainder" - a soft monetization design that leans on social/guild systems to reduce (but not eliminate) the paid-currency sink, worth comparing against Sword Forge's own economy design for social-assist mechanics.
