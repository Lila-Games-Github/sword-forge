Executive Summary

Sword Forge is a cozy crafting-shop sim. You play a blacksmith who explores a fogged map to discover magical traits, fuses them onto a blade through a chain of skill minigames (heat, hammer, quench, sharpen), forges a custom sword, and sells it to a rotating cast of customers — building gold, reputation, and a home.

The shipped prototype is a complete single-town loop with a full tutorial. The design ahead expands it into a town-to-town journey: the smith's hometown is destroyed, Town 1 is a survival story paced by a menacing landlord, and each subsequent town brings its own customers, storylines, materials, minigames, and décor as the smith chases the title of greatest blacksmith alive.

The fantasy

Master a craft with your hands — every sword is made, not bought from a menu — and become a legend of the forge.

The hook

Quality is earned through minigame skill, and who you choose to arm (good vs. evil) reshapes the town around you.

The vibe

Warm, unhurried, no player death — the tension lives in craftsmanship and moral choice, not danger.

2

Design Pillars

Craft is felt, not clicked

Every stage of forging is a hands-on minigame. Skill — not menus or timers alone — sets the outcome.

Craftsmanship has consequences

Sword quality decides customer outcomes; who you serve decides the town's fate. The blade is a moral instrument.

Cozy, never punishing

No player HP, no death, no game-over. The stakes are pride, reputation, and story — a game you relax into.

Depth through variety

Per-trait crafting, unique maps, and town-gated systems keep a simple loop from going stale.

A journey, not a treadmill

Towns give the grind a shape: arrive, build, uncover a story, move on. Progress carries with you.

3

Audience & Platform

Audience: fans of cozy crafting/management sims (PotionCraft, Stardew, Moonlighter, Papers-Please-style moral choice) — players who enjoy mastery of a satisfying loop and light narrative, in short-to-medium sessions.

Platform: web, mobile-first (touch + mouse), delivered as a single self-contained HTML file auto-deployed to GitHub Pages. Implemented

Session shape: a few minutes per customer; a "game day" is a handful of customers; a town is a long-form arc (~90–100 days, TBD).

Core Gameplay Loop

Built — loop-test

The ore→sword loop is now built as a PotionCraft-style navigation puzzle — the "Path-Forge" — in the Swordforge_new_looptest.html build. Crafting a sword is the exploration: you pilot a molten blade along a path to reach hidden traits, acquire them, then finish and sell it. (The older index.html still runs the legacy grid loop — see §7.4.)

The six-beat loop — Gather → Navigate (which is the craft) → Forge → Sell — with meta-progression (rent, reputation, town arcs) wrapping around it.
  1. Gather — collect ores by hand (a separate activity from crafting).
  2. Smelt & plan — pick ores (each a fixed-direction path segment), grind them at the stamp mill to extend reach, and add them to the smelter to lock the path.
  3. Navigate — bellow to set travel speed, pour the bloom to the anvil, hammer to travel the path, nudge with the dragon's fire to pull the blade precisely onto a hidden trait, then pour water from the mug to acquire it. Repeat to collect more traits.
  4. Forge — select a blade shape and hammer it into form.
  5. Sell — fulfil a customer's request for gold + reputation, or list in the passive shop.
  6. Reinvest — buy ores/materials, upgrade tools, unlock shape-maps & décor, pay rent.

Design / Sharpen / Polish sit between Forge and Sell as optional, purely cosmetic steps (see §11).

6

Good vs Evil Reputation

Planned (rep exists)

Reputation is the morality system. There is no neutral customer — every customer is either good or evil, and the player must judge which from appearance and request alone. There are no markers.

The moral 2×2. Refusing a good customer hurts, exactly as serving an evil one does.

The spiral

Reputation feeds back into the customer pool: low reputation summons more evil customers and unlocks more evil storylines. Stay on the evil path and the town itself slowly corrupts and is ultimately destroyed — the "corruption exit" of §5. High reputation keeps the town healthy and its good storylines flowing.

Current vs planned A numeric reputation with payout multipliers is already Implemented (sale +1, refuse −1; Rep<0 ×0.8 payout, Rep>10 ×1.2). The good/evil customer axis, refusal-of-good penalty, evil-influx, and corruption ending are Planned layers on top.

Exploration & Maps

Built — loop-test

Exploration and crafting are merging into one PotionCraft-style navigation puzzle. The grid is gone: you pilot a molten blade along a path across a hand-drawn map to reach hidden traits, and the ores you choose are the path.

7.1 The Path-Forge map Built — loop-test canon

Ores stack into a planned route; the hammer travels it, the dragon's fire pulls the blade back to sit exactly on a hidden trait, and water (the mug) acquires it.
Ore = path
each ore draws a fixed-direction path segment (no rotation yet — rotation ingredients come later). Stack many ores to plan a longer route toward a trait.
Grinding
grinding an ore at the stamp mill before smelting extends its segment — raw ore reaches ~50%, fully ground up to 100%, scaled by grind amount.
Speed (bellow)
bellowing heats the ore → sets travel speed. The ore cools over the run so speed decays: hot = fast but imprecise, cold = slow but exact.
Travel (hammer)
pour the bloom to the anvil and hammer for smooth forward movement along the path.
Align (dragon fire)
a movable dragon breathes fire on press-and-hold; when the fire lands on the anvil it slowly drags the blade + its route back toward centre — a controlled pull you can stop partway (it never resets). The fine-alignment tool for sitting exactly on a trait.
Acquire (water)
aligned on a hidden trait, pour water from the mug over the anvil to acquire it (this also quenches the ore, heat → 0). Traits stack — collect several per run, then repeat plan → travel → acquire.
Quality How precisely you land on a trait sets its quality (align distance → tier: ≤9 Epic · ≤20 Fine · ≤34 Weak) — the speed↔precision trade-off is the core skill (see §10). The world is a 2800×1960 fog-of-war map; every trait shows a ? until acquired (see §8).

7.2 Hazards & the sword's HP Concept

Cozy stakes The map has hazard zones. The smith has no HP — but the active sword does: drifting the molten blade through a hazard damages it, and at 0 HP it shatters (losing the in-progress blade & its traits). No player death, no game-over — only lost work-in-progress. Routing around hazards is part of the planning puzzle. Loop-test status: hazard zones are drawn but not yet wired to a sword-HP system — currently decorative.

7.3 The 24 shape maps Concept

  • 1 main map — the trait map above (discover the 24 traits).
  • + 24 shape maps — one per trait, each holding that trait's 10 blade shapes. All 24 are global & fixed and each looks entirely different. Discovering a trait unlocks its shape map, which is then bought from a trader.
Scale 24 traits × 10 shapes = 240 shape unlocks, across 25 bespoke maps — see the scope risk in §26. The legacy 50 g per-shape unlock may coexist or be retired (TBD).

7.4 Legacy: the 50×50 grid Current build — to be replaced

The shipped game still runs a 50×50 fog-of-war grid (seed 1337): the smith starts at centre (25,25), moves in 8 directions (each costing a metal), dashes 1–3 cells via the hold-and-release Purify minigame, and takes −20 hazard damage only on the cell landed on. This is the loop the Path-Forge map replaces.

8

Traits & Discovery

Built — loop-test

24 traits, each hidden on the map under fog of war — 8 signature traits at the ore-path ends + 16 scattered far out. Every trait shows a ? until acquired. The sword's travel clears fog over an area, exposing nearby ? marks; aligning on one shows a tier-coloured ring and its alignment tier but not its identity. You reach a trait by navigating the path (§7) and acquire it by pouring water from the mug once aligned — which reveals its real symbol (permanently) and adds it to the blade. Traits stack. Alignment sets the tier (§10); each ore path ends at a fixed signature trait (copper→Sharp, iron→Durable, gold→Noble, aluminium→Flexible; ember→Flame, frost→Ice, tide→Water, gale→Storm).

Current build (legacy): a trait is discovered by stepping on its grid cell and fused via the heating minigame in §9. Its per-trait "difficulty tiers" below describe that legacy minigame; in the new model, per-trait uniqueness shifts toward the crafting chain and bespoke materials (§12).

9

Heating — Two Kinds

Built — loop-test

Two distinct fires, neither of which sets quality (quality comes from alignment, §10). Acquiring is done with water (the mug), not heat:

Bellow-heat → speed

Working the bellows heats the ore before it travels, setting travel speed (speed = max(0.24, heat/100) × base). The ore cools once out of the furnace (~100→0 in 10s), so it slows as it goes — the fast-vs-precise dial; re-insert to re-heat. Built

Dragon-fire → pull / align

A movable dragon breathes fire on press-and-hold; when it lands on the anvil it slowly pulls the blade + route back toward centre — the controlled fine-alignment tool. Built

Acquire = water With the blade aligned on a hidden trait, pour water from the mug over the anvil to acquire it (also quenches the ore). See §7.1 / §8. The old "fire to acquire" concept was replaced by the water/mug quench.

Legacy heating minigame Current build (index.html) — to be replaced

The shipped game fuses a trait with a stabilize-band minigame: Pulley (lower the bucket) → Bellows (tap +16, or hold +40/s vs. 18/s decay) → Stabilize in the green band (default 40–70 for 2.0 s). A per-trait countdown (≈7.5–14 s; tutorial 45 s) set quality: stabilize in time → Epic, else Fine. In the new model, quality comes from navigation precision (§10) instead.

10

Quality System

Built — loop-test

Quality comes from one thing: navigation precision — how exactly you land the blade on a hidden trait when you acquire it. Three tiers by align distance: ≤9 Epic · ≤20 Fine · ≤34 Weak (beyond 34 = not on the trait).

Hot/fast travel overshoots; cold/slow travel + the dragon's pull lands exactly. Each trait is scored on its own landing (at the water-quench), so a run can mix qualities.
Consequence Design, Sharpen, and Polish no longer affect quality — they're purely cosmetic (§11). The whole verdict is made during navigation.

Legacy: the four-stage chain Current build — to be replaced

The shipped game sets quality via a chain: heat outcome → sword-HP cap (≥80 Epic / 40–79 Fine / <40 Weak) → hammer penalty (−1 tier / 2 misses) → sharpen over-hone (−1 tier / 10% past 110%). Navigation precision replaces all of it.

Value tiers (unchanged, from summed trait value): I Common (<30) · II Uncommon (<60) · III Rare (<90) · IV Epic (<120) · V Legendary (≥120). A trait-less sword = 5 g. Overlays render cracks on Weak and sparkles on Epic swords.

Forging & Finishing

Shape + Hammer built

By the time navigation (§7) ends, the blade already carries its traits and quality. Finishing is now short — shape it, then optional cosmetics.

Traits & quality are locked in during Navigate. Shape + Hammer finish the blade; Design / Sharpen / Polish are optional cosmetics.
  • Shape — tap the orb on the anvil (a tap, not a drag) to pick a blade shape. Cosmetic, but some customers request a specific shape. Shapes are earned from a trait's shape-map (§7.3). Loop-test: 3 placeholder shapes (Shortsword / Longsword / Broadsword)
  • Hammer — the shaping minigame: hammer the blade into the chosen form over 2 stages × 3 timed targets (hit each shrinking ring before it fades). Distinct from the navigation hammer; the hit/miss tally is currently cosmetic. Built (ported from swordforgeV2)
  • Design (optional · cosmetic) — grip / guard / pommel art; trait-skinned parts for Flame/Ice/Water.
  • Sharpen (optional · cosmetic) — a grindstone flourish; no longer affects quality.
  • Polish (optional · cosmetic) — applies a polish pigment / finish to the blade.
Retired The old cosmetic Quench step is gone — "water" (the mug) is now the trait-acquire quench during navigation (§7 / §8). Fine-alignment is the dragon's fire pull.

Materials & Gathering

Redesign

12.1 Ores — the path ingredients Built — loop-test

Ores are the heart of the loop. Each ore type draws a fixed Bézier path segment; the smith stacks them to plan a route (§7). The loop-test ships 8 ores: 4 "up" (Copper, Iron, Gold, Aluminium) and 4 "down" curvier ones (Ember, Frost, Tide, Gale), each ending at a signature trait (§8). Grinding an ore at the mortar before smelting extends its reach (~50% raw → 100% ground). The smelter then melts it into a glowing orb that travels the path. Later: rotation ingredients that bend a path's direction. (placeholder gem icons for Ember/Frost/Tide/Gale)

12.2 Gathering Planned — the leaning direction

A separate activity Ores are gathered by hand (PotionCraft-style), not auto-generated — a distinct activity from the crafting/navigation puzzle. This replaces the shipped smelter's passive metal generation.

12.3 Per-trait crafting materials Planned

Some traits will demand bespoke inputs on top of ore — e.g. a fire crystal for a Flame blade — sourcing per-trait uniqueness into the recipe. New materials are also a per-town unlock track (§5). Sourcing (gather / buy / town-specific) is TBD.

12.4 Legacy: metals & the smelter Current build — to be replaced

The shipped game uses 8 metals as directional movement fuel (4 free cardinals + 4 diagonals at 50 g), auto-generated by the smelter at 1 metal / 10 s (lowest-first, upgradeable +1/tick per 50 g). Ores + gathering replace this.

Automation & Hiring

Concept

To counter fatigue over a long game, the smith hires help to automate the rote parts of the loop — reserving the hands-on navigation puzzle for the swords that matter (the "variable depth" principle, §26).

Gathering

A hand collects ores for you.

Grinding

The stamp mill runs itself.

Smelting & bellows

An assistant smelts the bloom and works the heat.

Navigation

Auto-run a recorded path for routine swords (like today's Auto-Craft).

Finishing

A striker & grinder handle shape / sharpen / polish.

Open design Cost/gating (gold? town unlock?), whether automation trades quality for convenience, and — the delicate one — how much of the navigation puzzle (the core skill) can be automated without hollowing out the game are all TBD.

Customers & Day Cycle

Day system Implemented: 7 customers per day (sales and refusals both count); after the 7th, tap End Day to advance via a "Day N" fade transition. Gold, Vault, metals, reputation, shop, and blueprints persist across days.

Requests: scripted openers (#1 "any", #2 Flame, #3 ice-dragon) then random from #4; 10% of requests also demand a specific shape. Payout = sword value × reputation multiplier.

Planned layers

  • Variable customer count — festival, holiday, and "war week" days raise or lower the number of arrivals. Planned
  • Per-town casts — each town has its own customers and storylines (the one thing that doesn't carry over). Planned
  • Good/evil customers — see §6. Planned
Out of scope here The named-customer story campaign (character arcs, quality→fate, dialogue) is being drafted separately in the story/ folder and is intentionally excluded from this GDD.

Recorded Compositions

Implemented

Players save a blade's trait-set + material recipe as a named composition, keyed by trait-set. Re-recording an existing set offers Update (replace) or Keep Both (a second recipe for the same traits). The ⚡ Auto-Craft button runs the full forge pipeline from a composition (prepending a heat minigame); performance sets one uniform quality across the sword's traits.

Technical Architecture

Single self-contained index.html — all HTML, CSS, and vanilla JS in one file; no build step, bundler, or framework. Global mutable state + direct DOM manipulation. Auto-deploys to GitHub Pages on push to main. Implemented

Save / load — required, not built Nothing persists across reload today. With 90–100-day towns and full cross-town carry-over, save/load is mandatory: it must persist gold, Vault, materials, compositions, upgrades, unlocks, tutorial progress, day/town state, reputation, décor, and (eventually) premium entitlements — with a version/migration story. This is the single biggest technical prerequisite for the town system. Planned — top priority
Scale watch The single-file model is a real constraint against the planned content volume (24 unique maps, 240 shapes, per-town assets). Asset delivery and file size will need a plan even if the code stays single-file.

Risks

⚠ Content scale

24 bespoke shape-maps + 240 shapes + per-trait recipes + per-town casts/materials/minigames + a décor layer is an enormous surface for a small team on a single-file build. The top risk. Mitigate with templating, prioritisation, and staged town releases.

⚠ PotionCraft clone risk

The redesign shares PC's core loop. Without a hard push on the smith identity (ore→bloom→blade, heat-as-speed, hammer/water) it reads as derivative. Lean into what's ours.

⚠ Navigation depth & feel

The whole game now rests on the path puzzle being satisfying and learnable. The variable-depth + automation plan (reserve full navigation for masterworks) is the countermeasure — validate it.

⚠ Rent vs. earn balance

~1,000 g/month against ~15–100 g/customer needs a designed earn curve, or rent becomes a grind wall or a non-event.

⚠ Save/load dependency

The whole town system is blocked on persistence. Build it first.

⚠ IAP in a cozy game

Real-money cosmetics can clash with cozy trust; needs a static-build storefront solution and strict no-pay-to-win guardrails.

Bloom
the molten mass smelted from ore, poured to the anvil, that travels the path.
