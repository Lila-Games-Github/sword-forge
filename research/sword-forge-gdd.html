Sword Forge

A cozy 2D blacksmith crafting game: explore for magical traits, forge them into custom swords through hands-on minigames, and sell to the townsfolk — as you rebuild a life, one town at a time.

Executive Summary

Sword Forge is a cozy crafting-shop sim. You play a blacksmith who explores a fogged map to discover magical traits, fuses them onto a blade through a chain of skill minigames (heat, hammer, quench, sharpen), forges a custom sword, and sells it to a rotating cast of customers — building gold, reputation, and a home.

The shipped prototype is a complete single-town loop with a full tutorial. The design ahead expands it into a town-to-town journey: the smith's hometown is destroyed, Town 1 is a survival story paced by a menacing landlord, and each subsequent town brings its own customers, storylines, materials, minigames, and décor as the smith chases the title of greatest blacksmith alive.

The fantasy

Master a craft with your hands — every sword is made, not bought from a menu — and become a legend of the forge.

The hook

Quality is earned through minigame skill, and who you choose to arm (good vs. evil) reshapes the town around you.

The vibe

Warm, unhurried, no player death — the tension lives in craftsmanship and moral choice, not danger.

2

Design Pillars

Craft is felt, not clicked

Every stage of forging is a hands-on minigame. Skill — not menus or timers alone — sets the outcome.

Craftsmanship has consequences

Sword quality decides customer outcomes; who you serve decides the town's fate. The blade is a moral instrument.

Cozy, never punishing

No player HP, no death, no game-over. The stakes are pride, reputation, and story — a game you relax into.

Depth through variety

Per-trait crafting, unique maps, and town-gated systems keep a simple loop from going stale.

A journey, not a treadmill

Towns give the grind a shape: arrive, build, uncover a story, move on. Progress carries with you.

3

Audience & Platform

Audience: fans of cozy crafting/management sims (PotionCraft, Stardew, Moonlighter, Papers-Please-style moral choice) — players who enjoy mastery of a satisfying loop and light narrative, in short-to-medium sessions.

Platform: web, mobile-first (touch + mouse), delivered as a single self-contained HTML file auto-deployed to GitHub Pages. Implemented

Session shape: a few minutes per customer; a "game day" is a handful of customers; a town is a long-form arc (~90–100 days, TBD).

4

Core Gameplay Loop

The six-beat loop. Meta-progression (rent, reputation, town arcs) wraps around it.
  1. Gather — accumulate metals/ores (auto-generated today; hands-on gathering planned).
  2. Explore — spend materials to move across a fogged map and discover traits.
  3. Heat — play the heating minigame to fuse a discovered trait onto the active blade.
  4. Forge — shape → hammer → quench → design → sharpen the sword into the Vault.
  5. Sell — fulfil a customer's request for gold + reputation, or list in the passive shop.
  6. Reinvest — upgrade the smelter, unlock metals/shapes, buy décor, pay rent, save compositions.

6

Good vs Evil Reputation

Planned (rep exists)

Reputation is the morality system. There is no neutral customer — every customer is either good or evil, and the player must judge which from appearance and request alone. There are no markers.

The moral 2×2. Refusing a good customer hurts, exactly as serving an evil one does.

The spiral

Reputation feeds back into the customer pool: low reputation summons more evil customers and unlocks more evil storylines. Stay on the evil path and the town itself slowly corrupts and is ultimately destroyed — the "corruption exit" of §5. High reputation keeps the town healthy and its good storylines flowing.

Current vs planned A numeric reputation with payout multipliers is already Implemented (sale +1, refuse −1; Rep<0 ×0.8 payout, Rep>10 ×1.2). The good/evil customer axis, refusal-of-good penalty, evil-influx, and corruption ending are Planned layers on top.

A fog-of-war grid. The smith starts at the centre (25,25). 8-directional movement costs 1 unit of a direction-specific metal; a hold-and-release Purify dash minigame extends a move to 1/2/3 cells for the same single metal. Hazards damage only the cell landed on (crossing over is safe).

7.2 The cozy shift — sword HP, not smith HP Planned

Design change from the shipped build Today the smith has an HP bar and can "die" (which shatters the blade). The intended cozy model: the smith has no HP at all. Instead, the active sword has HP and can be destroyed — hazards damage the sword, and at 0 HP the sword shatters (losing the active blade & its fused traits). No player death, no game-over — only the loss of work-in-progress.

7.3 The 24 shape maps Concept

A planned expansion of exploration:

  • 1 main map — holds the 24 traits to discover (today's grid).
  • + 24 shape maps — one per trait, each holding that trait's 10 blade shapes to explore for. All 24 are global and fixed (they never change town-to-town), and each of the 24 looks entirely different.
24 traits × 10 shapes = 240 shape unlocks. See the scope risk in §26.

Traits & Discovery

Implemented

24 traits, each at a fixed map coordinate. Stepping on a trait cell reveals a ❓; heating it fuses the trait. A trait's base value scales with distance from centre: value = floor(distance / 35.355 × 100) → 0–100. A trait can be fused onto a blade only once.

Each trait has a unique heating minigame (§9), grouped into three difficulty tiers:

TierBehaviourTraits
A — Staticfixed band, tuned position/width/decayFlame, Ice, Floral, Sharp, Balanced, Fury, Durable, Endurance, Heavy, Cruel, Grace, Blood
B — Dynamicbands oscillate, gust, jitter, split, or repositionWater, Celestial, Flexible, Storm, Savage, Luck, Accurate, Cursed
C — Sequential / hiddenmulti-stage or hidden-marker "by feel"Noble, Honor, Lightning, Dark
Planned extension Per-trait crafting will go beyond the heat band into a unique full process — e.g. Flame needs higher heat + a flammable polish + a fire crystal in a late stage. See §12. Planned

Heating Minigame

Implemented

Triggered on a discovered trait: Pulley (tap to lower the bucket) → Bellows (tap +16 heat, or hold at +40/s vs. 18/s decay ≈ +22/s net) → Stabilize in the green band for a cumulative time.

Heat timer → quality: stabilize before the countdown ends → the trait fuses at Epic; if the timer expires first it still fuses, at Fine (one tier down, no hard fail). Timer length is per-trait (e.g. Grace 7.5 s, most static 9 s, dynamic/staged ~11 s, Durable 11.5 s, Endurance 14 s).

10

Quality System

Implemented

Three tiers — Weak (+0) · Fine (+10) · Epic (+20) value bonus — set by a four-stage chain:

Weak is the floor. Stage 2 caps quality by HP (≥80 → Epic, 40–79 → Fine, <40 → Weak) — now the sword's HP under the cozy model.

Value tiers (from summed trait value): I Common (<30) · II Uncommon (<60) · III Rare (<90) · IV Epic (<120) · V Legendary (≥120). A trait-less sword = 5 g. Overlays render cracks on Weak and sparkles on Epic swords.

11

Forging Pipeline

Implemented
* Quench is skipped when the last-fused trait is Flame. Auto-Craft runs the whole chain with a prepended heat minigame.
  • Shape — pick 1 of 10 blade shapes (3 free, 7 unlock for 50 g each). Shapes are cosmetic, but some customers request a specific shape.
  • Hammer — hit 6 shrinking-ring targets over 2 stages; misses cost quality (−1 tier / 2 misses).
  • Quench — a feel-only cooling beat (no quality effect); skipped for Flame blades.
  • Design — pick grip / guard / pommel art (cosmetic); trait-skinned parts for Flame/Ice/Water.
  • Sharpen — slide the blade across a grindstone to a 0–120% bar; 90–110% is the "keen" no-penalty window, over-honing past 110% costs −1 tier / 10% over.

Materials & Gathering

12.1 Current: metals & the smelter Implemented

Eight metals double as directional movement fuel — 4 cardinal (steel/iron/magnesium/bronze, free) + 4 diagonal (titanium/aluminium/blackIron/redIron, 50 g each to unlock). The smelter auto-generates 1 metal / 10 s, feeding the lowest-count metal until all pooled metals reach 5, then uniformly at random. Only unlocked metals generate. Upgrade for +1/tick per 50 g.

12.2 Planned: hands-on gathering Planned (leaning in)

Direction Inspired by PotionCraft's crystal gathering, the smith would gather materials/ores by hand rather than have them auto-generate. Leaning toward replacing auto-generation with gathering — the two are documented as alternatives until decided. final call TBD

12.3 Planned: per-trait crafting materials Planned

Beyond metals, traits will demand bespoke inputs — e.g. a flammable polish and a fire crystal for a Flame sword. New materials are also a per-town unlock track (§5). Sourcing (gather vs. buy vs. town-specific) is TBD.

Automation & Hiring

Concept

To counter minigame fatigue over a long game, the smith can hire help to automate steps of the craft. All five hands-on activities are automatable:

Heating

An assistant works the bellows.

Gathering

A hand collects materials/ores.

Hammering

A striker shapes the ingot.

Quenching

Cooling handled for you.

Sharpening

A grinder finishes the edge.

Open design Cost/gating (gold? town unlock?), whether automation trades quality for convenience, and how it interacts with per-trait unique processes are all TBD.

Customers & Day Cycle

Day system Implemented: 7 customers per day (sales and refusals both count); after the 7th, tap End Day to advance via a "Day N" fade transition. Gold, Vault, metals, reputation, shop, and blueprints persist across days.

Requests: scripted openers (#1 "any", #2 Flame, #3 ice-dragon) then random from #4; 10% of requests also demand a specific shape. Payout = sword value × reputation multiplier.

Planned layers

  • Variable customer count — festival, holiday, and "war week" days raise or lower the number of arrivals. Planned
  • Per-town casts — each town has its own customers and storylines (the one thing that doesn't carry over). Planned
  • Good/evil customers — see §6. Planned
Out of scope here The named-customer story campaign (character arcs, quality→fate, dialogue) is being drafted separately in the story/ folder and is intentionally excluded from this GDD.
16

Recorded Compositions

Implemented

Players save a blade's trait-set + material recipe as a named composition, keyed by trait-set. Re-recording an existing set offers Update (replace) or Keep Both (a second recipe for the same traits). The ⚡ Auto-Craft button runs the full forge pipeline from a composition (prepending a heat minigame); performance sets one uniform quality across the sword's traits.

UX / UI

Implemented

Three swipeable screens: Shop (0) · Counter (1) · Forge/Map (2). A gold/reputation pill and Day badge sit on both the Counter and Forge screens. Inventory (Vault) and Recorded Compositions open as scrollable modals (tile grid → drill-in detail).

Feedback & juice: damage/heat flashes, gold-pulse on passive sales, "trait acquired" & "New Trait Discovered!" toasts, metal-toss-into-bucket on a spend, guided hand pointers, and parchment gameAlert popups for warnings. Key modals: shape, hammer, quench, design, sharpen, record, compositions, inventory, chart/minimap, tutorial-review.

Onboarding

Implemented

A 4-scene animated intro → a data-driven tutorialFlow that walks the full first craft (select metals → reach a trait → heat → record composition → forge → sell → meet scripted customers → learn the chart & Purify dash), plus post-tutorial guides (shop unlock at 500 g; a one-off "record your compositions" reminder). Technical Architecture

Single self-contained index.html — all HTML, CSS, and vanilla JS in one file; no build step, bundler, or framework. Global mutable state + direct DOM manipulation. Auto-deploys to GitHub Pages on push to main. Implemented

Save / load — required, not built Nothing persists across reload today. With 90–100-day towns and full cross-town carry-over, save/load is mandatory: it must persist gold, Vault, materials, compositions, upgrades, unlocks, tutorial progress, day/town state, reputation, décor, and (eventually) premium entitlements — with a version/migration story. This is the single biggest technical prerequisite for the town system. Planned — top priority
Scale watch The single-file model is a real constraint against the planned content volume (24 unique maps, 240 shapes, per-town assets). Asset delivery and file size will need a plan even if the code stays single-file.

Comparison — Potion Craft: Alchemist Simulator

Our closest reference: a cozy crafting-shop sim where you brew potions by steering a vial across a hand-drawn map using ground ingredients, then serve customers with moral weight. Here's where Sword Forge stands.

ExplorationNone — the "map" is the crafting surface.A real world map to explore for traits, + 24 planned shape maps.Better. We add an adventure layer PC lacks — at the cost of two systems to unify.
Customers & moralityDeep: haggling, reputation, serve/refuse shady requests.Simpler payout multiplier today; planned good/evil axis that reshapes the town.Worse now, bolder later. PC's economy is richer; our morality-drives-the-town is more consequential.
Progression / metaAlchemy-machine skill tree, recipe learning, shop upgrades — complete.Upgrades + blueprints today; town-to-town journey planned.Worse now, bigger ambition. PC is polished & finished; our town spine is a larger structure, unbuilt.
Materials / gatheringGrow a garden, buy from merchants, experiment to ID ingredients.Auto-generated metals today; hands-on gathering planned.Worse now. Auto-gen is passive; gathering (this doc's lean) closes the gap.
Art & presentationCohesive hand-drawn woodcut, mature, atmospheric.Custom forge art, still cohering; distinct theme.Worse now. PC is a shipped, unified art bible; ours is prototype-stage but has a clear identity.
FeelMeditative, zen, slow.Active, hands-busy, minigame-driven.Different flavour. A more energetic cozy — a genuine niche of its own.
Platform / modelPremium paid, PC/console/mobile.Free web + cosmetic IAP.More accessible, but a cosmetic-IAP model is harder to get right in cozy — needs care (§22).
Takeaway Sword Forge's edge is the explore-then-craft adventure loop and morality that reshapes the world — things PotionCraft doesn't do. Its risks are exactly PC's strengths: cohesion, gathering depth, and finished polish. Prioritise gathering, per-trait uniqueness, and a unifying art pass to trade on our strengths while covering the gaps.

Risks

⚠ Content scale

24 bespoke shape-maps + 240 shapes + per-trait crafting chains + per-town casts/materials/minigames + a décor layer is an enormous surface for a small team on a single-file build. The top risk. Mitigate with templating, prioritisation, and staged town releases.

⚠ Minigame fatigue

A ~2-minute loop repeated across ~650 customers/town will wear thin. Per-trait uniqueness and automation are the intended countermeasures — validate they're enough.

⚠ Rent vs. earn balance

~1,000 g/month against ~15–100 g/customer needs a designed earn curve, or rent becomes a grind wall or a non-event.

⚠ Save/load dependency

The whole town system is blocked on persistence. Build it first.

⚠ IAP in a cozy game

Real-money cosmetics can clash with cozy trust; needs a static-build storefront solution and strict no-pay-to-win guardrails.

⚠ Two-system cohesion

Explore-vs-craft risks feeling like two games; a unifying loop/art pass matters (PotionCraft's lesson).
